# Phrasing

`FST-DICT-DESIGN.md` §7 names the dictionary the format exists for, and it is the
one that cannot be a dictionary file: 32,768 clause settings times 631 right-bank
enders is 20.7M chords, and §7 measures a tenth of that at 150MB of baked JSON.
Here it is a grammar, in 881KB, and the whole single-stroke language lives in it.
It was ported from the user's `phrasing.py`; that file is gone and these three are
the source:

| file | what it holds | who owns it |
|---|---|---|
| `dictionaries/phrasing-subjects.xfst` | the subject map, 16 sets of `T P W H` | hand-written; change the left bank here |
| `dictionaries/phrasing-lexicon.xfst` | the right bank, satellites, copula chords, conjugation | generated by `scripts/gen-phrasing-lexicon.py` from `~/.config/plover/phrasing_table.json`, which the mining pipeline in `~/.config/plover/phrasing/` writes |
| `dictionaries/phrasing.xfst` | every rule | hand-written |

`~/.config/plover/PHRASING-DESIGN.md` is the design these implement.
`phrasing-resdesign`, at the repo root, holds only what is not yet built: the
right bank's redesign. `docs/phrasing-rhs.md` fills it in from the corpora and
lists what is still to decide.

## The keys

```
🦐  held clause          #  the gate: every phrasing stroke has it
^   full form           +  question order          @  not phrasing
S   "but"   SK "and"    K  "if"                    R A O  modality
T P W H  the subject    *  negation                E U    aspect
```

`#` is what makes a stroke phrasing, and no other dictionary has it: see
"`#` is phrasing's".

| `T P W H` | subject | | `T P W H` | subject |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `T` | I | | `TW` | that |
| `P` | you | | `PW` | this |
| `H` | he | | `PWH` | what |
| `PH` | she | | `WH` | the {noun} |
| `TP` | it | | `TPW` | a(n) {noun} |
| `TH` | we | | `TWH` | to (infinitive) |
| `TPH` | they | | `TPWH` | there |
| none | no subject, 3sg: `#-B` "adds" | | `W` | no subject, plural: `#W-B` "add" |

The two with no subject need a verb. There is no plural bit: "these", "those",
"one", the wh-words but "what", the passive and the imperative are gone. A clause
takes one conjunction: `S` is "but", `SK` "and" and `K` "if", with any subject.
"if" takes no question, no infinitive and no clause without a subject.

"and if" was `SK`. It is gone, "and" moved from `S` to `SK`, and "but" took `S`.
`SK` is the theory's "and", `SKP`, without the `P`. Before a subject, "but" is
as frequent as "and" in web+news (9,382 and 9,353), and "and if" is 382. `K`
stays "if" (6,598), since a "that" before a subject is a verb's complement and
the right bank writes it.

## Three tapes, and who owns each

```
#^TP*  Chord    [FULL][SUBJ]it[/SUBJ][C][3sg][NOMOD][NEG][SIMPLE][PRES][V0]
       Chain    [FULL][SUBJ]it[/SUBJ][C][3sg][NEG][PRES][F:be]
       Arrange  it is not
```

`Chord` reads keys and writes a feature bundle. `Chain` reads a bundle and builds
the auxiliary chain: which auxiliary is finite, which are not, and what form the
main verb takes. `Arrange` reads a chain and writes English: agreement,
contraction, and question inversion.

The split is what makes each layer small enough to read. Nothing in `Chain`
mentions a key and nothing in `Arrange` mentions an aspect, so `Arrange` states
the contraction rules once over eight finite auxiliaries instead of once per corner
of the aspect-by-modality space. Both middle tapes are internal; the compiler
prunes their symbols, so a host sees keys to text.

`Chord` needs one hoist, where the prototype needed several. Its bundle is in key
order, and the mood keys `^ +` are the first keys, so they are the first features.
The one key out of place is `K`, which sits between `T` and `P` on the board:
`KFirst` reads it before `T`, so the subject keys `T P W H` arrive together and
the subject map is a map over four keys.

## The paradigm is a rule, not a table

The prototype generated the finite words as one table per surface form, keyed by
the whole selector, and the extension of that was nine tables of up to 96 rows
each. Those tables are the paradigm written out rather than stated, so the
paradigm moved into `phrasing.xfst` instead, and the generator kept only what is a
finite map by nature: the subject map, the right bank (629 chords), and the
conjugation (108 verbs times five forms).

The finite verb is now a few dozen hand-written rows -- `am`, `is`, `are`,
`was`, `were` and their negatives and clitics, in tables keyed by only what each
word depends on -- plus seven lines that say which auxiliary is finite:

```
  [ Modal(KSim)                Cls(VBase,     {be})    ]   ! she would go
| [ Modal(KPf)  SHave          Cls(VPastpart, {been})  ]   ! she would have gone
| [ NoMod(KPf, "[F:have]")     Cls(VPastpart, {been})  ]   ! she has gone
| ...
```

Each line is one corner of modality by aspect. The two arguments travel together
because they are one fact: the element that takes the finite form also fixes the
form of everything after it, so the corner that picks `have` is the corner that
makes the main verb a past participle.

Three tables became rules on the way, and each was storing something the bundle
already determined:

- **The clitic** comes from the finite auxiliary, so it is `(Fid, Agr, Tense)`
  and not a column. That deleted the `is_launch` special case: an adverb
  launchpad takes no clitic because `do` has none, and the guard was asserting
  what the table already said.
- **Which negatives are two words** is `am`, present `shall` and `may`, so the
  negative table is simply partial there and the separate `not` is what falls
  out. No set of selectors to keep in step.
- **The mids** are polarity-independent, because only the finite head takes a
  negative form. One table, not two.

## Modality is one dimension of eight

`R A O` are one value, because modals do not stack, and `-D` is its past:

| keys | present | `-D` |
|---|---|---|
| none | -- | -- |
| `A` | can | could |
| `O` | shall | should |
| `AO` | will | would |
| `R` | may | might |
| `RA` | must | used to |
| `RO` | have to | had to |
| `RAO` | going to | was going to |

"may" and "must" are modals like "can": they take aspect, need no `do`, and
take no subject clitic. Only "mustn't" contracts, so "may not" and "might not"
are two words. "must" has no past, so `RAD` holds "used to", and `RAU` is
"must have" and reduces to "must've" the way "might've" and "could've" do.

"going to" is a modal whose finite word is `be`: `Modal` gives it `[F:be]` and
a `[M:going]` mid, so it agrees and inverts with nothing new ("am I going to
go", "isn't going to"). Aspect goes to the main verb: `#TRAOUG` is "I'm going
to have gone".

"have to" and "used to" are verbs, and `Semi` says so before `Chain` sees them:
`[HAVETO]` becomes `have` with a `[TO]` clause after it, and `[MUST]` in the past
becomes `use`. So agreement and do-support are the lexical verb's: "she has to
go", "doesn't have to go", "do I have to go", "didn't use to go". "have to"
takes the aspect itself, `#TROUG` "I've had to go"; "used to" passes it to the
main verb, `#TRAEGD` "I used to be going". The clause after "to" is the
infinitive's, so an empty slot there is "be": `#TRO` is "I have to be".

The semi-modals are still in the verb bank as well. Freeing them is the right
bank's redesign, not this one.

## The infinitive and the clause with no subject

`TWH` is the nonfinite "to" clause. It has no subject and no agreement, so
modality, tense and the question have nothing to inflect and are ignored.
Negation is "not to", and "and" or "but" goes before it.

`#-B` and `#W-B` name no subject and need a verb: "adds", "add", "can add",
"are adding". `[NOAUX]` is what tells `Arrange` the clause opens on a word
rather than on a subject.

## Contraction is a priority union

Reduction is a preference, not a case: take the clitic where the tables allow
one, else the contracted negative, else the full form. `PU` says that in the
order it happens, and the two modes need no marker to tell them apart.

```
| Red(PU(Clit([ IsAff .o. SufW ], Tail),
         PU(Decl(Merge, Tail),
            Decl([ IsAff .o. AffW ], Tail))))
```

Three preferences deep, and the middle one is the modal perfect: "could've" when
nothing else claimed the auxiliary, which is what `this could've` needs and
`I'd have` does not.

## Question inversion, on a tape where the selector comes after the subject

The finite word leads a question and the selector that picks it is four symbols
*after* the subject. That is a hoist, the same one the record language uses for
its own flags: guess the symbol before the subject, read it after, and let
nondeterminism make the guess free.

```
define Pull(X, P)  [ [ 0 .x. X ] P [ X .x. 0 ] ?* ] ;
define Invert  [ PullFid .o. PullTns .o. PullPol .o. PullAgr .o. PullAnd ] ;
```

Four symbols, fifteen branches, composed. "and" and "but" come along, because
they lead the whole clause: `#+STK` is "and am I", not "am and I", and `#+ST`
"but am I".

## The hole: the verb is the word you write next

`-RG` names no verb. It says the main verb of this clause is whatever the next
unit writes, so the clause is stroked first and the word after it:

```
#TPERG  RUPB   ->   it's running
#TPURG  TKPW   ->   it's gone
#TP*RG  RUPB   ->   it doesn't run
#+TP-RG RUPB   ->   does it run
#ERG   RUPB   ->   is running
```

Nothing in `Chain` or `Arrange` knows about it, and that is the point of putting
it here rather than in a dictionary of phrases. The hole is a **lemma**: the
chord maps to `[V:_]`, and the five form relations map `[V:_]` to the tail that
asks the host for that form. So the corner that would have chosen a past
participle chooses `{:infl:en}`, and agreement, aspect, modality, question
inversion and do-support keep working with nothing added.

A base form asks for nothing at all, because the word as written *is* the base
form: `#TPARG` is "it can" and the record ends there.

The tail is `{:infl:…}` and not `{^ing}` because a lexicon knows that the past of
"run" is "ran" and the participle is "run", and a rule knows neither. `en` is a
mode of its own rather than something `ed` works out from the words before it,
because whatever chose the corner already knew.

### What it cost

One verb moved, and the reservation lives in `gen-phrasing-lexicon.py` rather
than in the mined table, because the pipeline that writes that table rewrites it.

| chord | was | is |
|---|---|---|
| `-RG` `-RGD` | hear | **the hole** |
| `-RGT` `-RGTD` | hear about | hear |
| `-RPGT` `-RPGTD` `-RPGTZ` | free | hear about |

`-RG` is two keys, and every two-key chord in the right bank is a top-100 verb
except this one: main-verb "hear" is rank 96, and the grammar handles auxiliary
"have" and "be" itself. `reassign` checks each destination against the copula
chords as well, because those are not in the verb table. `-RGZ`, hear's `+Z`
past, follows it to `-RGTZ`.

## A held clause: the next stroke goes before the verb

`🦐` on a clause holds it open where an adverb goes, and whatever the next stroke
writes lands there. No board has the key: a long press on `plover-hid-timing`
sends it, so holding the chord is holding the clause.

```
🦐#T-R     OFPB      ->   I often work
🦐#TUR     TPHEFR    ->   I've never worked
🦐#T*R     AULS      ->   I don't always work
🦐#+TP-R   OFPB      ->   does it often work
🦐#TWH-R   HRAOERL   ->   to really work
🦐#TROB   OFPB      ->   I often have to add
```

The gap is after the finite word and before the rest of the chain, and where
the chain has no finite word before the verb, it is before the verb. `Chain`
says it with `Gap`, an optional `[GAPAT]` at that one position in each corner,
and `GapOnce` keeps the paths where a `[GAP]` bundle placed exactly one. After
`Arrange` the gap is a `[HOLD]`, so it closes the way the held subject's hole
does: the rest of the clause is the tail.

It waits for a stroke and not a word, because a tail waits for text: a stroke
that writes two words puts both in the gap. A gap with nothing after it holds
nothing, so `🦐#T` then "often" is "I'm often".

This replaced two things. The adverb enders were eight right-bank chords that
each wrote an adverb and held the verb, and the pre-verb satellites put
"just", "also", "never" or "really" before a particular verb ("just want",
"never mind"). Both are gone from the table. A held clause reaches every adverb
in the theory, before every verb, and costs no chord. What the adverbs gave up
is free, and the copula chords below took three of those chords.

One record waits for one unit, so a held clause takes no held subject and no
`-RG` verb.

## `^` is the full form, and what it does where nothing contracts

`^` asks for the full form: `#^TP` is "it is", `#^T*` "I am not". A clause with
no auxiliary has no full form to undo, so `^` gives it one: `do`, which is what
`@` used to be for.

```
#^T-G     ->   I do go
#^T-GD    ->   I did go
#^-B      ->   does add
#^TROG   ->   I do have to go
#^TAG    ->   so I can go
#^STKAG  ->   and so I can go
#^STAG   ->   but I can go
#^TWH-G   ->   in order to go
#^TWH*G  ->   in order not to go
```

`Chain` reads `[FULL]` as a reason for do-support, as it reads a question. "have
to" and "used to" are lexical verbs by then, so they take it too.

A modal clause that reduces to nothing shorter writes "so" before its subject
instead, after any "and". "but so I can go" is not English, so `^` writes no
"so" after "but". `Contracted` is every branch of `Arrange` that the reduced
mode prefers to the full form, so a clause outside its domain reads the same in
both modes, and `SoMark` gives a `[SO]` there to a statement whose
subject is a word of its own. "so" before a question, after "if", or before
"what" or a held noun is not what `^` asked for, so those keep the no-op:
`#^TKAG` is "if I can go", and `#^PWHAG` "what can go". So do "was" and
"were", which have no clitic and are not modal. An infinitive is always the same
in both modes, and its full form is "in order to".
## `^+` is a question tag

`^+` is a full-form question, and an affirmative question has no contraction for
`^` to undo, so `^+` asks for a tag instead:

```
#^+TP-B   ->   it adds, doesn't it
#^+TP*B   ->   it doesn't add, does it
#^+TEG   ->   I'm going, aren't I
#^+TROG  ->   I have to go, don't I
#^+TRAGD ->   I used to go, didn't I
#^+TP     TPHAOEUS  ->   it's nice, isn't it
#^+T-GZ   SKAOL     ->   I go to school, don't I
```

The tag is the clause's finite word with the polarity flipped and the subject as
a pronoun: this, that and a held noun are "it", and "I" borrows "aren't". `*`
still says the clause's own polarity, which gives up the full negative question
"does it not add"; it was rare. "may" has no negative contraction, so its tag
is "may I not". A clause with no verb, or one ending on a satellite, is waiting
for a word, so its tag is the tail of the record and lands after the next
stroke. "what", an infinitive, a clause that already holds something, and a
clause with no subject take no tag, and `^+` there is still the full question.

`dictionaries/phrasing-tag.xfst` builds the tag as a second clause on the chain
tape, appended after `[TAG]`, and hands it to `Arrange` as a reduced question.
It is its own dictionary, above `phrasing`, because of what it costs. The tag
is written last and depends on the subject, so the transducer carries the
pronoun, the polarity and the finite word across the whole right bank. Carried
through the main grammar, that made the artifact 36 MB. As its own dictionary
only a `^+` stroke pays, and the main artifact stays under 1 MB. The tag
dictionary is 21 MB forward and 20 MB reverse, which is most of the stack.

Two details of the construction are about the determinizer rather than the
grammar. The pronoun is found by a replace rule that puts a one-symbol marker
beside the subject, and `PushPron` moves the marker to the end, because matching
the subject word from the end makes the determinizer track every subject at
once. And each stage runs over `Bundle.l` rather than over every string, so no
stage expands `?*` across the whole alphabet. A waiting tag's `[ATTACH_L]`
belongs to its tail, so this grammar skips `Canon`, which would hoist the flag
to the end of the record.

## The copula with an article

The empty verb slot is the copula, and three chords are that slot with an
article after it. They are one family, `F-TS` with the middle key saying which:

| chord | writes | e.g. |
|---|---|---|
| `-FPTS` | a | `#T-FPTS` I'm a |
| `-FBTS` | the | `#TH-FBTS` we're the |
| `-FGTS` | an | `#PW-FGTS` this is an |

`-D` is the past, as it is on the slot alone: `#T-FPTSD` is "I was a". Under an
auxiliary the copula has to surface, because the article needs something to
follow: `#TUFPTS` is "I've been a" where `#TU` alone is "I've". `Cls` takes the
form of `be` each corner gives a verb for that. The chords live in
`phrasing_table.json` as `copula`, so the pipeline keeps them out of the verbs'
way and stenodrill sees them.

## The other hole: the subject's noun is the word you write next

A held subject writes the determiner and holds the rest of the clause, so the
noun goes in the middle:

```
#WH-B     KAT   ->   the cat adds
#TPW-B    KAT   ->   a cat adds
#TPW-B    EG    ->   an egg adds
#WHEB    KAT   ->   the cat is adding
#+WH-B    KAT   ->   does the cat add
```

`TPW` is one chord for "a" and "an", because the record says "a or an, by the
next word": `[IF_NEXT]pattern[THEN]an[ELSE]a[END]`, Plover's `{=…}`, with the
pattern `AEU` in `user.json` uses. The host decides it when the noun writes.
Until then it shows "a", because the tail waiting for the noun is not the text
the choice reads. Plover's `{=…}` reads the next action, and the tail's preview
is that action, so the plugin puts the preview on the end of both answers rather
than after them. `tests/hole/article.strokes` holds the cases, and
`scripts/check-hold.py` replays them through Plover.

The mechanism is the tail, and the subject map is where it is stated: the
subject is `[SUBJ]the[HOLD][/SUBJ]` with no `[C]`, because "the cat's" reads as a
possessive. `Arrange` writes the subject and then the space before the next
word, so the hole is always followed by that space and everything after it is
the tail. One rewrite closes it:

```
the[HOLD] is adding    ->   the[AFTER]is adding[END]
is the[HOLD] adding    ->   is the[AFTER]adding[END]
```

Question inversion needs nothing of its own, because the subject is still
followed by the space that the finite word left. A hole with *nothing* after it
holds nothing, since there is no tail to wait with: `#+WH` is "is the", and the
noun follows it as ordinary text.

One record waits for one unit, so a clause cannot have two holes. A held subject
therefore takes no `-RG` verb and no `🦐`: `#WH-RG` has no entry, and
`tests/golden/phrasing.tsv` asserts it.

## `#` is phrasing's

`#` gates every phrasing stroke, and no other dictionary in the stack has an
outline that holds it. `recipe.toml` says so with `owns = "#"` on `phrasing` and
`phrasing-tag`, and the compiler holds every other dictionary to it: a grammar
or a flat dictionary that accepts `#` fails its checks, because its source is
ours to change, and an imported JSON entry with `#` is left out and counted,
because its source is not.

So the number bar is gone from the stack. Typey Type's digits, times, fractions
and numerals (437 entries) and the one `#` entry in `user.json` are left out of
their imports; Emily's literal numbers lost their 256 bit, `emily-modifiers` its
16 bit on digits and F-keys, Shrimple its capitalised syllable, and the folds
read no `#` stroke. `@` is the modifiers' namespace, and `#@` with it.

Measured over `tests/golden/phrasing.tsv` as the probe list. The frontier is the

writer's time, so this is not a problem.

## Both directions

```
$ stfst reverse build/dicts/phrasing.stfst --system systems/stenotype-extended.toml
it's                 #TP      #TPU     #TPE     🦐#TP    ...
it is not            #^TP*    🦐#^TP*  #^TP*E   🦐#^TP*E
we weren't           #TH*D    #TH*ED   🦐#TH*D  🦐#TH*ED
in order not to be   #^TWH*   #^TWH*D  #^TWHR*  #^TWHA*  ...
I'm going to         #TEGZ    #TRAO    #TRAORG
```

"it's" has several outlines because the progressive and the perfect scaffold to
the same word, and both are real entries. "I'm going to" is the progressive of
the verb "go to" as well as the modal, and the cheapest-first search offers the
verb first. The infinitive ignores modality, so "in order not to be" has one
outline per modality.

## Checked

`tests/golden/phrasing.tsv`, 4,797 cases. The file is the expectation, and the
sample is chosen the way the grammar is split:

- **One case per ender chord**, all 631 of them. That is the part that is a
  table, and a map is checked by reading every row of it once.
- **A prime stride through the clause dimensions** and through the rest of the
  ender table, so the sample cycles every subject, conjunction, modality,
  polarity, aspect, tense, mode and question rather than landing on one corner
  of each. That is the part with rules in it.
- **Modality**: each of the eight in both tenses, both polarities, the question,
  `^` and every aspect.
- **The held subjects**, each dimension once, and their two exclusions as
  no-entry rows: a clause that already holds its noun cannot also hold its verb.
- **The cases the design names**: the sixteen subjects, the conjunctions, `^`
  over every kind of clause, `@`, and the held clause.

The whole language is 20.7M strokes and no file holds it. The file was recorded
from this grammar and read: a line that changes is a change to what a clause
writes. `tests/golden/phrasing-tag.tsv` is the same for the tag.

The port from `phrasing.py` cost four bugs and this file's coverage caught all
four. The clausal "and" landed after an inverted finite word. The infinitive
branch never consumed the mood flags, so the negative infinitive had no entry.
Questions had no entry at all until the selector hoist went in. And `define
Record` in the grammar shadowed the prelude's own `Record`, which silently
emptied the reverse direction -- the compiler now refuses a grammar that
redefines a name a prelude macro reads, because `Fresh` is expanded after the
grammar and read a name that no longer named a record.

## The retro modifier layer was retired

`phrasing-retro.xfst` folded a run of modifier strokes into the clause before
it, newest first, and was checked but never in the stack, because no host hands
a dictionary its own run in that order. The redesign took its modifier keys for
the left bank and replaces it with a different mechanism, so it was deleted with
its recipe and golden file. Git history holds it, and the measurements that
made its case: the tape order a fold needs is the reverse of the order a writer
strokes.
