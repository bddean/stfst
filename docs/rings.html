Rings

A steno keyboard for two thumbs, made of nested rings. One rule decides everything: entering a live cell takes it. Everything else on the board is ground you can travel through.

The rule

A cell is live if steno order still allows its key. A cell is ground if you already took it, if steno order has passed it, or if it holds no key at all. The hub is ground once you have left it.

That is the whole mechanism. Nothing waits on time, distance or turning, so there is no threshold to tune and nothing fires early or late.

The rules that look like extra rules are consequences of it. Sliding out to the next ring "erases" the ring behind, because steno order killed those keys and dead cells are ground. Turning through a ring adds keys, because you cross them. And the shortcut is the middle: two cells far apart on one ring are joined by a straight chord through the hub, and the hub is ground. A comb needs portals because it is flat. A ring has a middle.

Both hubs are pies of complete answers: landing takes the whole choice, so the hub is ground from then on and you can leave at any angle. The left hub holds S and T because they are the two keys before the walk proper, and taking T out of the ring is worth 3.6 points on its own — every T-to-a-far-key chord (TR TH TW TPR STR) becomes a straight run out. What is left is five cells at 72°, and T's old seat is a corridor.

Each ring is six slots wide with a gap between every pair, and the gaps line up, so a straight run from hub to rim takes nothing. That corridor is load-bearing: 12.4% of strokes cross the right ring 1 taking nothing (-ES, -ET), and 2.7% cross the left ring 1 taking nothing (SA-).

A key can hold more than one cell, and the keys that need it are the floats. Steno order never kills a float, so the float ring is live all stroke, and a thumb that has to travel round it to find the one it wants takes every float on the way. So each float is repeated: twice round the left rim, three times round the right. Run out any spoke and you arrive in a gap between two floats, a flick either way from both. The third left float is one spoke over, reached under the rim through ring 2, which by then is dead.

The vowels repeat for the same reason, which is why A O goes round three times rather than sitting in one place: whichever consonant you leave from, a vowel is straight out and the other one is next door.

The pinky end

-T -D and -S -Z are one finger's four keys, and the corpus says what it does with them: one key at a time, nearly always. Across 340k strokes, -TS is 2351 and -DZ is 1466, while -TZ is 26 and -SD is 3.

So do not spend four cells on four keys. Spend two, repeated round the ring like the vowels, and put the voiced key beyond its plain one: -D is straight out through -T, -Z is straight out through -S. That is the pinky reaching down, drawn.

It reads like a mode and it costs no mode. Nothing rewrites anything, radial still means later in steno order, and every combination is still a set of cells: -TS and -DZ are one step round, -TD and -SZ are one step out, and the two rare crosses are a diagonal. The ring that had four keys in four of six slots now has two keys everywhere, which is what the 12.4% of strokes that cross ring 1 taking nothing needed: they arrive on a spoke, and from a spoke both are next door.

A bank of sticky keys — tap now, add to the next stroke — would buy what a mode leaves out. Nothing here needs one: every key of both banks is reachable in one stroke, and the worst case is a chord that costs two motions instead of one. Worth keeping in the drawer.

The right hub is a pie of four complete answers, so landing on one makes the whole hub ground and you can leave at any angle. EU gets a slice of its own because it is as common as E alone.

The rings are superellipses, not circles: (|x/a|^p + |y/b|^p)^(1/p) with p≈3.5. One formula for the drawing and the hit test, and it fills a rectangular bank instead of wasting the corners.

The ring order is the steno board, folded

Scoring every cyclic order of the left consonants against 340k strokes, the best is T P H R W K, and it is not arbitrary: it is the keyboard's two rows joined at both ends. Moving T to the hub leaves the same loop with a gap where T sat.

   T P H          T ─ P ─ H          S spans both rows on a steno board,
   K W R    →    ╱         ╲         and T is the only key between S and
   (S spans)     K ─ W ─ R           the walk, so both are the hub.

The right bank folds the same way: F R B G L P for the first three columns, and the last two columns fold radially instead — see the pinky end below.

What the shortcut face buys

Entering a cell against the walk can yield a different key, which is a second label on a cell rather than a new mechanism. Two candidates:

  • pair chord. The backward face of a top-row cell is its column: T→TK, P→PW, H→HR.
  • opposite cell. The backward face is the cell across the ring: in TPHRWK that is T↔R, P↔W, H↔K.

The residual without either is entirely reaching H or R from the far side of the ring: PR TR KR KH TH KHR TKR TPR STR. Switch the selector and watch which of those go green — and watch what goes red.

Both lose. A face is not free, because the backward side of a cell is already doing a job: it is how you cross the dead zone. Give a cell a second label and crossing it backwards takes that label instead of nothing.

  • pair chord changes nothing on the left and costs 16 points on the right. Under steno order the backward face of an early key is unreachable: if you are entering T backwards you already hold a later key, so T is dead and TK is dead with it.
  • opposite cell does buy PR and KH, and it costs PW, TK and KWR: 89.7% down to 72.3%. Across the corpus, backward travel over a dead cell carries 9.3% of all steps, and a face on a dead cell would fix 3.1%.

So the ring has no spare surface. The middle is the shortcut, and it costs one straight line.

Not carried over

The modifier drag ring, with out / out-far / out-back, is three thresholds: a distance, a reversal, and where the second distance ends. Those are the knobs the comb deleted, and each is a thing that can fire early or late. The float ring is cells instead, reached along a corridor. A sector is a place you can see; a depth is a gate you have to feel.

What survives from that idea costs nothing: a float advances no steno order, so crossing back inward leaves the rings as they were and the stroke goes on.

The portals go with the comb. Their whole job was to reach a key many cells ahead in one motion, and the middle of a ring does that for free.
